# Miami Vice - Salt Water: music recommendations

Sound supervision notes for the storyboard draft. The film is a two-hour feature set in
1988 Miami, and it is built on three layers: an **original synth score** in the Jan Hammer
register, **five licensed period songs**, and **diegetic source music** in almost every
location. Cues are listed in `src/data/miami-vice-music.json`, keyed to scene ids.

## The approach

The show's rule was: score the interiors, songs the driving, silence the moments that
matter. This draft keeps that rule.

- **Interiors are scored, never songed.** The bullpen, the warehouse, the bank and the
  county office have no tune in them at all - only fluorescent buzz, rain, radios and a
  held low pad.
- **Songs are for movement.** Four of them, all of them canonical Miami Vice, and every
  one is placed in a car, on a causeway or over a shot of the city.
- **Little Havana is never scored.** Every Little Havana scene plays source music: a
  cafecito radio, a ball game, a body shop with a boom box.
- **The loudest moment is followed by silence.** The raid peaks in the racking, then drops
  to nothing for Prieto's answer. The standoff on the causeway is the only place a drum
  break is allowed to do the acting.

## Score palette (original cues)

Fretless bass, Prophet and Fairlight pads, a gated snare used sparingly, steel drum for
anything to do with money, and a sax-like synth lead that belongs to Burnett and never
resolves until he does. Tempos stay low: nothing in this film is in a hurry except the
raid.

Main title: a two-note ostinato called **Salt Water**. It returns, faster, under the raid,
and resolves only over the last shot of the bay.

If licensing is impossible, commission five originals in the same register and keep the
placements: a dawn driving song, a temptation song, a hotel-suite song, a two-man driving
song, and a standoff song with a drum break you can cut a gun lowering to.

## Source and diegetic music

- Docks (S03): salsa on a watchman's radio, playing to an empty office.
- Little Havana (S11, S37, S58, S90, S106): cafecito radio, Latin pop, a call-in show.
- Bookie's back room (S13, S73): a ball game at low volume.
- The Carlyle (S21, S55): a house pianist playing the same tune both times.
- Dog track (S44-S46): PA announcer and crowd.
- Hotel Miramar (S75, S77): a cover band playing something fast and wrong, cut dead on
  the loading bay.
- End (S122): a car radio crackling into the next case.

## Sound design that does the work

Rain (the docks, the storm, the causeway), fluorescent buzz (the bullpen), the truck's
cooling unit cycling (the Everglades), tape hiss (Crockett's recorder below decks),
hazard lights ticking (the causeway, three times), a shovel in dirt (the airstrip), and a
ship's horn (the river).
