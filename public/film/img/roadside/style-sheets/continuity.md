# The Roadside Inn (scenes 30–49) — continuity lock

Fresh set for *Nobody's Witness* on the Scarlet Reel. **Nothing in the shipped film is replaced:**
`public/film/img/s30/`–`s49/`, `src/film/story.json` and `src/film/studio-edits.json` are untouched.
All new work lives in `public/film/img/roadside/`.

Loosely following the screenplay (scenes 30–49), rebuilt so the geography holds: the arrival, the
night, then **Jack escaping through the inn** — up the corridor, out the bathroom window, down onto
the kitchen roof, in through the open back door, forward to the lobby to get Mr. Noda, back to the
kitchen, out to the yard and away in the old pickup. Screenplay beats that fight that route are
re-staged, never dropped.

Scene **36 (Vera's apartment)** is deliberately out of scope: it is not the roadside inn.

* **Upstairs:** the stairs from the lobby land **halfway** along the upstairs corridor, which runs
  front-to-back over the ground-floor one, **shoji paper screens along the RIGHT wall**, room doors
  on the left.
  * Toward the **front**: **Jack's room, the end door.** Its window looks down over the **gravel lot
    and the highway** — that is how he sees the four cars arrive (39).
  * Toward the **back**: **the bath, the other end door.** Its one high window is in the **back
    wall, above the deep wooden tub**, and looks straight down onto the **shallow tin roof of the
    kitchen extension** — that is the way out (42, 43).
  * So: **Jack sleeps at the front and escapes from the back.** The men come up the stairs between
    him and the way out, and he runs past the landing, away from them, to the bath — the dead end
    that is not one, because of the window Mrs. Noda showed him.
* **The gutter** runs along the low edge of the kitchen roof. **The kitchen's back door** is in the
  rear wall of the extension, directly under that edge — Jack drops off the gutter and swings
  through it into the kitchen. It was standing open all evening (scene 37).
* **The pantry** is the low latched door on the left of the corridor at the kitchen end.
* Everything the script needs is within a few steps of everything else: bedroom → bath → roof →
  back door → kitchen/pantry ↔ corridor ↔ lobby.

## How to reference (house rule for this pass)

The shipped frames were used **sparingly, for identity only** — Jack's face and the inn's shell, so
the man and the building are the same ones the film already plays. Composition, lighting and
staging come from this set's own sheets, not from the old frames; the old shots' framings must not
be copied. In practice: at most one shipped frame in a call, and only where a face or a facade has
to match, never as a composition to copy.

## Look

Photorealistic contemporary Japanese neo-noir, 16:9 film stills, 1920×1080. Cold slate/blue-green
shadows and rain, warm amber practicals inside, wet reflective surfaces everywhere outside,
restrained 35mm grain, natural exposure, subtle highlight halation. **No legible signage, text,
logos, number plates, watermarks or borders** (the inn's sign reads as a shape, not words; the
vending machine is unbranded). No film grade, no volumetric light shafts — the builder strips both.

## Screen direction of the escape (fixed)

1. **Upstairs:** Jack leaves his room at the front, hears the boots on the stairs below as he passes
   the landing, and keeps going **away from them, toward the bath at the back**. The gunman reaches
   the landing and fires down the back half of the corridor; the holes stitch along the **right-hand
   shoji screens**, always the right wall, always receding away from the stairs. Jack's room window
   behind him looks down on the lot; the bath window in front of him is the way out.
2. **Out:** bath window (back wall) → tin roof → gutter → **open back door** → kitchen.
3. **Forward:** kitchen → corridor → lobby mouth (light switch on the wall beside him) → dark →
   across the lobby to the counter, where Mr. Noda is hiding → **back** down the corridor.
4. **Out again:** pantry for the couple → back door → yard → pickup → farm gate → track → highway.

Jack moves at believable urgency: fast purposeful strides, coat swinging with the body, breathing
hard — never sprint-posing. He is 57, not 27. The men are always a room or a flight behind him,
never in the same frame as a clean shot at him.
